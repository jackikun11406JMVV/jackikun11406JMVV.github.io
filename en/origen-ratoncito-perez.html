Coloma’s Pérez

What was Little Mouse Pérez like?

Luis Coloma was the first to leave a detailed and recognisable written description of Pérez.

Coloma presents him as “a very small mouse, with a straw hat, gold spectacles, raw-canvas shoes and a red satchel”.

He then adds that he was a “very worldly mouse”: a little gentleman used to carpets, elegant homes and dealing with all kinds of people.

His hat, spectacles and red satchel make him easy to recognise even before the adventure begins.

Pérez’s home

Where did Little Mouse Pérez live?

The Pérez family has a tiny home in Madrid, very close to Calle del Arenal.

Arenal 8, Carlos Prast’s confectionery shop

The Pérez family home is at number 8 Calle del Arenal, beside Carlos Prast’s confectionery shop and facing a great pile of Gruyère cheeses.

The whole family lives inside a large Huntley biscuit box: Mrs Pérez, Adelaida, Elvira, Adolfo and, of course, our elegant little mouse.

They have tea, music, good manners and a home full of tiny mouse-sized details.

The magic of the adventure

Yes: Bubi turns into a mouse

Coloma’s story is full of magic and adventures far greater than a simple exchange for a tooth.

When Bubi asks to accompany Pérez, a “wonderful marvel”takes place: after a sneeze caused by Pérez, the young king is transformed into a little mouse.

He can then travel through Madrid’s pipes, visit the Pérez family home and accompany him through a night that will change the way he sees the world.

How we built the story · +12

History, visual recreation and story: three different levels

This section is for adults and older readers. Here we explain what comes from historical sources, what we completed visually and what belongs to The Origin of the Little Mouse Pérez.

We are not trying to fake a historical photograph. We want to show, honestly, how we interpret today the details Coloma left in writing.

Some images are symbolic scenes. For example, showing Coloma writing beside our representation of Pérez helps tell the relationship between author and character visually, but it does not present that scene as a documented historical meeting.

Three levels to keep history and story distinct

Documented

What appears in Coloma’s text, the manuscript or reliable historical sources.

Visual recreation

How we visually complete what Coloma did not specify: clothing, spaces, gestures or the composition of certain scenes.

In our story

The idea behind the story · +12

What if Coloma had met the Pérez family in Jerez?

This is the creative question behind Juan Manuel Vela Vacas’s story.

The starting point

Luis Coloma was born in Jerez and was the first to leave a detailed and recognisable literary description of Pérez. The story takes that real link with the city and poses a fantastic possibility: that, as a young man, Coloma might have met a family of little wine-cellar mice there.

Within The Origin of the Little Mouse Pérez, that meeting later becomes the connection between Coloma, Bubi and the Pérez family.

Documented history

What do we know about Pérez in literature?

Here we enter the more literary side: antecedents of the name, the manuscript, dates and early editions. Here we gather literary and historical information for adults and curious readers without changing the magic with which children experience the tradition.

The name was already in circulation

Spanish literature contains earlier traces. Benito Pérez Galdós uses the expression “ratoncito Pérez” in La de Bringas, written in 1884, several times as a nickname for Francisco Bringas. Studies of popular literature collected by the Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes also point to mentions of Ratón Pérez in texts and traditions associated with Fernán Caballero.

For that reason, it is more cautious to say that Coloma took up a pre-existing name and tradition and gave them new narrative life.

Read La de Bringas on Cervantes Virtual

Coloma’s decisive contribution

The autograph manuscript of Ratón Pérez , preserved by the Real Biblioteca, is dated approximately to around 1894 and contains a dedication to Alfonso XIII. The Real Biblioteca explains that Coloma wrote the story for the young king, whom his mother affectionately called Bubi.

Coloma thus made Pérez into a character with a home, family, personality, adventures and a moral role within a story written for one particular child.

Author’s note · +12

Why I wanted to tell Pérez’s story this way

When I began The Origin of the Little Mouse Pérez I wanted to bring together naturally Jerez, its wine-cellar tradition and Luis Coloma, but I also wanted to offer a simple explanation for the many differences between one family’s customs and another’s.

Coloma was born in Jerez and was the first to leave a detailed and recognisable written description of Pérez. That connection allowed me, in my story, to have the young Coloma meet a family of little wine-cellar mice and make Jerez an important part of the story.

The other fundamental idea was this: Pérez is not a single mouse; Pérez is a family. There are many Pérez mice around the world, and each can have their own customs. That explains why in some homes they leave coins, while in others they may leave sweets, small gifts or other treats.

🪙 Every Pérez can have their own customs
I did not want the story to suggest that there is only one correct way to experience the tradition. A coin, a sweet or a small gift can all be part of the same magic because each member of the Pérez family works in their own way.
🥛 An easier way to find the tooth
In the story I suggest leaving the tooth in a glass, cup or small container beside the bed. That makes it easy for Pérez to find and means he does not have to search beneath the pillow or get so close to the sleeping child, reducing the chance of waking them.

The intention is not to replace each household’s customs, but to give them a place within the same story. If every Pérez belongs to one great family and each member has their own way of working, all those small differences can coexist while still being part of the tradition.

This explanation stays in the +12 section because it discusses the author’s decisions and how the story was built. The family section keeps Pérez’s story and magic just as children experience them.

Historical curiosity

Carlos Prast or Carlos Prats?

The real owner of the confectionery shop was Carlos Prast. However, in Ratón Pérez Luis Coloma wrote the surname as Prats. That is why both forms can be found when researching the story.

In our illustration and in our story we keep Prast, the confectioner’s real surname, because this is a reconstruction within our own story rather than a literal reproduction of Coloma’s text.

The historical information is presented separately from the artistic recreations and the fiction of The Origin of the Little Mouse Pérez.
