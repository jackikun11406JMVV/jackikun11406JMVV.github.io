Sinterklaas: the Saint Nicholas tradition that sails from Spain.

Three places, three layers of the story

Where does Saint Nicholas come from?

The answer changes depending on whether we mean the historical figure, his relics or Dutch folklore. Keeping these layers apart makes the legend easier to understand.

01Patara and Myra

Tradition places Nicholas's birth in Patara and remembers him as bishop of Myra, both in present-day Türkiye. His memory became linked with helping children, sailors and vulnerable families.

02Bari

Relics attributed to Nicholas were transferred to Bari in 1087. This is why Western Europe often calls him Saint Nicholas of Bari, although he served as bishop in Myra.

03Spain

In Dutch folklore, Sinterklaas lives in Spain and arrives by sea. There is no single documentary explanation for this part of a tradition shaped over centuries.

The celebration in the Netherlands

The arrival begins weeks of anticipation.

From Spain to the Netherlands by steamboat

In mid-November, Sinterklaas is portrayed arriving from Spain on a steamboat. One city hosts the national arrival and many towns hold their own intocht, with music, parades and families waiting by the harbour or along the streets.

On land he wears a bishop's robes and continues on a white horse. The steamboat and much of today's imagery were popularised in the nineteenth century.

1

The intocht

The public arrival opens the Sinterklaas season and turns the harbour into a family celebration.

2

Schoen zetten

Children place a shoe near the fireplace, sing and often leave a carrot or hay for the horse. A small gift or treat may appear by morning.

3

Pakjesavond

On 5 December, families and friends exchange presents, read rhyming poems and open imaginative disguises known as surprises.

4

6 December

This is the saint's traditional feast day. After the main gift night, the story says that Sinterklaas returns to Spain.

What the celebration symbolises

Every object tells part of the tradition.

♜

Mitre and crozier

They recall Sinterklaas's origin in the memory of a Christian bishop, distinct from modern Santa Claus.

⚓

The boat

It turns his return into a collective journey and connects the patron of sailors with Dutch ports.

♞

The white horse

In popular imagination it carries Sinterklaas through streets and over rooftops while his helpers deliver gifts.

◒

Shoe and chimney

They express the idea of a secret night-time gift: helping without seeking recognition.

Aa

Poems and chocolate letters

They make gifts personal. Letters often match the recipient's initial and poems mix affection with playful teasing.

✦

Pepernoten and sweets

Spiced biscuits, marzipan and chocolate coins help create the shared atmosphere of the season.

A tradition that also changes

Who are the Pieten?

The Pieten help prepare and deliver presents and entertain crowds during the parades. Their depiction has changed. The historic Zwarte Piet portrayal is associated with racial stereotypes and has caused wide public debate; many contemporary celebrations use roetveegpieten, helpers marked with chimney soot, or more varied representations.

Traditions are not fixed: familiar symbols remain, while communities also reconsider them so celebrations can become more respectful and inclusive.
Spain, Alicante and the book

Does Sinterklaas really live in Alicante?

Dutch tradition says that Sinterklaas arrives from Spain, but there is no single accepted historical explanation or one universal, unchanging departure port. Alicante preserves and promotes a popular connection with the legend.

The book uses that connection as a creative starting point: it imagines Saint Nicholas living in Alicante and sailing from there each year. That journey, its characters and adventures belong to the story's fictional world.

Frequently asked questions

The essentials at a glance.

When is Sinterklaas celebrated in the Netherlands?

The season begins with the mid-November arrival. The main gift night, Pakjesavond, is 5 December; Saint Nicholas's traditional feast day is 6 December.

How does Sinterklaas arrive?

In Dutch folklore, he travels from Spain by steamboat and then rides through towns on a white horse.

What do children leave in their shoes?

They may leave a drawing, a letter or a carrot for the horse. They sing and hope to find a small gift, chocolate letter or sweets later.

Are Sinterklaas and Santa Claus exactly the same?

No. They share roots connected with Saint Nicholas and gift giving, but their appearance, dates and customs differ. Dutch tradition helped shape the later Santa Claus.
